"Right now in American society, one of the most heated debates is on affirmative action. How much we should give the underrepresented minorities or disadvantaged people some preference or preferential treatment? This has many different angles to look at the problem and it's a very complex problem. There are many different views but I am very much in support of affirmative action.

If we can all go into a totally colorblind society, that would be perfect but unfortunately we have not yet reached that stage. Even as an Asian-American, many people don't realize. In my case, they say 'you are chancellor of a great university.' I can tell you almost every day I have been somehow mistreated because of my color and my ethnicity. This is hard to understand for many people. That's why based on my personal experience we have not yet reached such a colorblind society.

Many people mention the sharp drop in [racially diverse admissions after ending affirtmative action] indicated that in the past we have given too much racial preferences in the admission process. That's not true. In fact, many of the students are highly qualified. We have many, many qualified students. Maybe from an academic score point, some students are just one point below someone else out of a 1,000 or 5,000 maximum score. I don't think that can distinguish people that much. We have to think in many other factors, that's where the race, ethnicity, and gender picture comes into it."

Exerpt from Sept 1996 interview with Chancellor Chang-Lin Tien, the first Asian and Chinese Chancellor of UC Berkeley and first Asian to lead a major US research university.
